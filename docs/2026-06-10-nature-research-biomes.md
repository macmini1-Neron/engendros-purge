## 1. True (typical) steppe — степ

**Dominant ground cover**
- Narrow-leaved turf (tussock) grasses dominate: feather grass (*Stipa*) + fescue
  (*Festuca*). Eight *Stipa* species widespread: *S. capillata* (hairy), *S. joannis*,
  *S. lessingiana*, *S. stenophylla*, *S. ucrainica*. [Encyclopedia of Ukraine — "Steppe" / "Feather grass"]
- Forbs play a **subordinate** role in typical steppe → less bright/colourful than
  northern meadow-steppe. Common forbs: *Ferulago galbanifera*, *Fragaria viridis*,
  *Jurinea arachnoidea*, *Potentilla incana*, *Salvia nutans*. [Encyclopedia of Ukraine — "Steppe"]
- Middle/southern steppe = xerophytic grasses (feather grass, esparto, fescue) +
  xerophytic forb admixture on southern chornozem. [Encyclopedia of Ukraine — "Steppe"]

**Heights / cover (sourced)**
- *Stipa pennata* (European feather grass): **0.60–0.90 m** high. [Wikipedia — Stipa pennata]
- Sandy steppe (Pannonian/Pontic, *Festuca vaginata*, *Koeleria glauca*,
  *Stipa borysthenica*): vegetation **sparse, max cover ~75%**. [EUNIS habitat 8091]
- Western Pontic steppe (west of Dniester): *S. lessingiana*, *S. capillata*. [EUNIS habitat 3759]
- Perennial herbs common, many **deep-rooted** (drought adaptation). [EUNIS 8091]

**Visual / late-spring–summer colour**
- Silvery-white feathery awns of *Stipa* shimmer/ripple in wind (signature look);
  base sward straw-green going to gold/buff as it cures through summer. [Encyclopedia of Ukraine; EUNIS 8091]
- Forb flecks of *Salvia nutans* (violet-blue nodding spikes) and yellows
  (*Potentilla*, *Ferulago*) scattered, not a dense carpet (subordinate forbs). [Encyclopedia of Ukraine]

**Ground texture / density / sightlines**
- Tussock (bunchgrass) structure → mounded grass clumps with bare/lichen-crust
  interspaces, NOT a closed lawn (cover capped ~75% even on sandy steppe). [EUNIS 8091]
- Tussock-to-tussock spacing in metres: **[UNSOURCED — do not invent]**.
- Sightlines: **long and open** — sub-metre sward height means a standing figure
  (1.7–1.8 m) is visible to the horizon; flat-to-gently-undulating relief. [implied by Stipa <0.9 m height, EUNIS/Encyclopedia]

## 2. Forest-steppe — лісостеп / lesostep (with shelterbelts)

**Structure / dominant cover**
- Temperate ECOTONE: landscape-scale **mosaic** of herbaceous grassland + woody
  patches; **10–70% arboreal cover**. [Erdős et al. 2018, Appl. Veg. Sci.; "Forest steppe" overview]
- Belt ~**9,000 km long × ~400 km wide**, Carpathian Basin → Russian Far East. [Erdős et al. 2018]
- Dominant trees: deciduous **oak (*Quercus robur*)** + lime (*Tilia cordata*);
  in Ukraine oak is in ~half the belt, with ash, maple, elm, linden. [Encyclopedia of Ukraine — "Forest-steppe"; Forest steppe overview]
- Wetter/north/lowland → denser *Quercus*/*Carpinus* groves; drier/south → sparser grassland. [Forest steppe overview]
- Ukrainian forest-steppe = gently undulating loess plain, **dissected by gullies
  and ravines (balky/yary)** — woody patches cling to ravines/north slopes. [Encyclopedia of Ukraine — "Forest-steppe"]

**Shelterbelts — полезащитні лісосмуги / полезащитные лесополосы**
(field-protective tree strips planted across the open arable forest-steppe; Russian
agroforestry references, **not a verbatim ГОСТ scan**):
- Most-effective design: **3 rows**, e.g. weeping birch (*Betula pendula*) or balsam
  poplar. [CyberLeninka — viability of field-protective belts]
- Common schemes: **3–4 rows** (pure single-species rows) or **4–5 rows** (main
  species centre + accompanying species edges). [studfile — конструкция ПЗЛП]
- Width: **longitudinal belts 12.0–15.0 m**, **transverse belts 9.0–12.0 m**;
  extra in-field two-row windbreaks **5–6 m** wide on fields >1 km. [studfile / agroforestry refs]
- Wider protective belts **30–60 m**, spaced at **300–400 m** intervals. [agroforestry refs]
- Function: trap snow, hold soil moisture, stop wind/dust erosion. [FSVPS 64; ru.wikipedia "Лесополоса"]

**Colour / texture / sightlines**
- Visual signature: tall, dense, dark-green linear tree strips forming a **rectilinear
  grid** across pale-gold cultivated/grassland fields — strong man-made geometry on
  open land. [derived from belt width/spacing above]
- Sightlines: open across fields BUT belts are hard visual/movement walls every
  300–400 m → broken into "rooms". [agroforestry spacing]
- Meadow-steppe grassland between belts is **brighter/more forb-rich** than true
  steppe (northern position). [Encyclopedia of Ukraine — "Steppe" contrast]

## 3. Pine bor — бор (Scots-pine forest interior)

**Trees**
- *Pinus sylvestris* (Scots pine), the foundation species of Eurasian boreal/subalpine
  pine forest, esp. on **low-fertility, dry sandy** sites. [Wikipedia/FEIS — Pinus sylvestris]
- Mature height **25–35 m** (typical), trunk to **~1 m** diameter; exceptionally
  **>45 m** on productive sites. [Wikipedia — Pinus sylvestris]
- Stand spacing/stems-per-hectare for бор interiors: **[UNSOURCED — do not invent]**.

**Ground cover (lichen-pine / зеленомошник–беломошник types)**
- Lichen pine taiga: *Pinus sylvestris* over a **very low, sparse dwarf-shrub layer**
  and a **ground layer dominated by *Cladonia* lichens** (*C. rangiferina*,
  *C. alpestris/stellaris*, *C. mitis* — "reindeer moss"). On podzols w/ thin raw humus. [EUNIS habitat 1283 — Lichen [Pinus] taiga]
- Associated dwarf shrubs/ground: *Arctostaphylos uva-ursi*, *Calluna vulgaris*,
  *Vaccinium vitis-idaea* (cowberry), *Cetraria islandica*; mosses *Pleurozium
  schreberi*, *Dicranum polysetum/spurium*, *Stereocaulon*. [EUNIS 1283]
- *Cladonia stellaris* forms **dense pale mats** dominating floor of late-successional
  oligotrophic (nutrient-poor, acidic) pine forest. [Kytöviita 2009, Functional Ecology]

**Colour / texture / density / sightlines**
- Floor reads **pale grey-green to whitish** (continuous *Cladonia* "беломошник" mat)
  or **deep green** (*Pleurozium* moss "зеленомошник"); cowberry adds dark-green
  glossy leaves + red berries. [EUNIS 1283; Cladonia refs]
- Trunks: tall, straight, bare lower boles with **orange-brown upper bark** —
  open, columnar "hall" feel; canopy high (25–35 m) and fairly open on dry sites
  (lichen woodland = open-canopy). [Wikipedia Pinus sylvestris; EUNIS 1283]
- Sightlines: **open at eye level** between bare trunks (sparse shrub layer); long
  horizontal views interrupted only by trunk columns. [EUNIS 1283 — sparse dwarf-shrub layer]

## 4. Riparian — river/stream bank floodplain forest (заплавний ліс)

**Trees / dominant cover**
- Banks of willow + alder forest with lush meadows and marshes (upper Dnieper
  riparian zone, RU/BY/UA). [RFE/RL — Dnipro/Kakhovka; WWF riparian forests PDF]
- Recognised type: **Poplar/Willow floodplain & gallery forest**. [WWF-Ukraine — riparian forests]
- Pioneer species: white willow (*Salix alba*) + poplars (*Populus*); alder (*Alnus*)
  on wetter banks. [WWF; RFE/RL]

**Growth rate / heights (sourced from Kakhovka regeneration)**
- Post-2023 Kakhovka drawdown: naturally regenerated willow forest grew
  **2–3 m in one year**; *Salix alba* now reaching **~6 m**; ~**150,000 ha**
  (≈930 sq mi) of former reservoir bed → largest floodplain forest in Europe. [RFE/RL; The Guardian/Caliber; Byline Times]

**Colour / texture / density / sightlines**
- Bright, fresh light-green willow/poplar foliage; fast, **dense thicket** growth
  → low visibility, tangled understorey, soft muddy/silty ground, standing water,
  reed/sedge fringe at the water line. [RFE/RL; WWF]
- Sightlines: **short and broken** — opposite of steppe; dense regrowth + braided
  channels. [derived from "thick forest"/rapid regrowth, RFE/RL]
- Linear, follows watercourse (gallery forest) — a green corridor through open land. [WWF — gallery forest]

## 5. Reed marsh / bog — плавні / болото

**Dominant cover**
- *Phragmites australis* (common reed): large perennial grass, height **1–3.5 m
  (or more)**, in **extensive dense stands** in/near shallow water. [Bugwoodwiki / USFS FEIS — Phragmites australis]
- Reed often displaces cattail (*Typha*) communities in fresh/brackish marsh and
  sedge communities in non-tidal marsh/wet meadow. [USFS FEIS — Phragmites]
- Dnipro плавні studied at Dnipro-Orilsky Reserve; reed density treated as a factor
  in in-channel hydraulics. [Biosystems Diversity — ecology.dp.ua 1253]

**Colour / texture / density / sightlines**
- Tall (to 3.5 m) **dense reed wall**, feathery purplish-brown plumes (late summer/
  autumn), bright green blades in summer → buff/straw when dead. [Phragmites refs]
- Ground = waterlogged, soft, standing/shallow water, no firm footing. [Phragmites — "in or near shallow water"]
- Sightlines: **fully blocked** at and above head height inside a reedbed (reed
  >3 m > a 1.8 m figure); navigable only by channels/open water lenses. [derived from 1–3.5 m reed height]

## 6. Carpathian alpine / subalpine flower meadow — полонина / polonyna

**Zone / structure**
- Polonyna = montane meadow in the **upper subalpine/alpine** Carpathian zone, long
  used for pasture. [Wikipedia — Polonyna]
- Subalpine zone: **1,100–1,400 m (north) → 1,400–1,900 m (south)**, almost pure
  Norway spruce (*Picea abies*) forest below, w/ rowan (*Sorbus aucuparia*). [Encyclopedia of Earth — Carpathian montane conifer forests]
- Above timberline (**~1,400 m NW → ~1,900 m S**): **krummholz** belt = dense
  thickets of dwarf mountain pine (*Pinus mugo*), dwarf juniper (*Juniperus communis*
  subsp. *nana*), green alder. [Wikipedia/EoEarth — Carpathian montane conifer forests]
- Closed forest → *P. mugo* krummholz ecotone at a belt **~1,430–1,510 m**;
  krummholz → alpine meadow transition **~1,700 m**. [Tatra/Carpathian study via search]
- *Pinus cembra* (stone pine) at the alpine timberline in highest ranges
  (Chornohora/Czornohora). [Carpathian treeline refs]

**Flowering species / colour by season**
- **Late April–spring**: continuous **carpet of lilac + yellow crocuses**
  (*Crocus heuffelianus*, Red Book of Ukraine). [starapravda — Carpathian flora]
- **June–July**: slopes covered with **bright-pink islands of rhododendron**
  (*Rhododendron myrtifolium / kotschyi*); scent like sour cherry. [starapravda; EUNIS 22414 — Carpathian Rhododendron heaths]
- Subalpine heath dominated by *Rhododendron myrtifolium* + *Vaccinium vitis-idaea*,
  w/ *Soldanella hungarica*, *Potentilla aurea*. [EUNIS 22414]
- Other showy subalpine forbs: edelweiss, gentian, primrose, alpine poppy. [starapravda]
- Peak colour **late spring → mid-summer** (purple crocus → pink rhododendron). [starapravda]
- Matgrass (*Nardus stricta*) swards typical of grazed polonyna: **[UNSOURCED — do not invent]** (Nardus is the classic polonyna grass but no metric sourced here).

**Texture / density / sightlines**
- Short, dense alpine turf + low (knee-/waist-high) shrub heath; **open, panoramic
  sightlines** above treeline broken only by krummholz thickets. [derived from EoEarth zonation + EUNIS heath]
- *Pinus mugo* krummholz = **dense, impenetrable low thickets** (movement walls). [EoEarth — "dense thickets"]

## 7. Ecotones — how neighbouring biomes grade (for world-blend zones)

- **Steppe ↔ forest-steppe**: gradual; woody cover climbs **0 → 10–70%** northward
  and into lowlands/ravines. On **loess** the edge is **diffuse** (ecotone richness
  intermediate, low species turnover); on **hard rock** a sharp **edge effect**
  appears. → blend steppe→forest-steppe softly over loess plains, sharply on rocky
  outcrops. [Erdős et al. 2018; Chytrý et al. 2022, J. Veg. Sci.]
- **Forest-steppe internal mosaic**: oak groves occupy **ravines, gullies, north-facing
  / wetter slopes**; grassland holds dry south slopes + flat loess. Topography drives
  the patchwork (not a clean line). [Encyclopedia of Ukraine — "Forest-steppe"; Erdős]
- **Bor ↔ steppe (sandy)**: Scots pine бор sits on poor **sandy** substrate, so it
  meets sandy-steppe (*Festuca vaginata* / *Stipa borysthenica*) on dunes/terraces —
  a substrate-controlled mosaic, pine clumps in sand grassland. [EUNIS 8091; FEIS Pinus]
- **Riparian ↔ marsh ↔ surrounding zone**: river → **reed/sedge fringe at waterline**
  → willow/alder **gallery forest** → meadow/marsh → upland biome. The gallery forest
  is a narrow green corridor that cuts THROUGH steppe/forest-steppe along water. [WWF; RFE/RL]
- **Carpathian vertical stacking** (clean elevation bands, S figures): spruce forest
  → krummholz (*P. mugo*) ecotone **~1,430–1,510 m** → alpine flower meadow **~1,700 m+**.
  Treeline is **higher in the south** of the range. [EoEarth; Tatra/Carpathian treeline study]
