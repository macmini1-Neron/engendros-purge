# Ground vegetation + water-edge plants — sourced sizes (metres)

Research date: 2026-06-10. For ENGENDROS PURGE Ukrainian/Russian steppe + meadow + water-edge biome
dossiers. All dimensions in METRES (cm given where the source uses cm). Each fact carries a short
source tag. Where a number could not be sourced it is flagged "NOT SOURCED" — not invented.

Coordinate reminder for modelers: world up = +Y, ~1 unit ≈ 1 m. "Height" below = vertical extent;
"clump width" = horizontal footprint of one tussock/cushion.

---

## 1. Feather grass — ковыль (Stipa spp.)

The Pontic steppe's signature tussock grass. Two relevant species:

### Stipa pennata (European feather grass / ковыль перистый)
- Foliage/culm height: **0.60–0.90 m** (60–90 cm). — *botanical ref: Wikipedia "Stipa pennata"*
- Habit: tufted perennial, silvery-grey feathery flowering spikes (awns). — *Wikipedia "Stipa pennata"*
- Awn (feathery plume) length: **NOT SOURCED** to a precise figure here (commonly cited ~0.2–0.35 m
  but no source captured this run — treat as unverified).

### Stipa capillata (ковыль-волосатик / needle grass)
- Culm height: **0.30–1.00 m** (stems 20–70(–100) cm). — *Wikipedia "Stipa capillata" + RBG Kew GrassBase*
- Awn length: **0.13–0.23 m** (12–18(–23) cm), hairy/scabrid — NOT feathery (the only Ukrainian
  needle grass with a non-plumose awn). — *tiligul.com biodiversity entry; Red Book of Ukraine*
- Habit: densely tufted, forms dense sods/tussocks. — *Red Book of Ukraine; Missouri Botanical Garden*
- Clump width: **NOT SOURCED** (described only qualitatively as "dense sods").

Modeling note: a feather-grass tussock reads as a ~0.3–0.9 m mound of fine blades with longer
(0.6–1.1 m total incl. awn) silvery seed plumes arching above. Colour: green-grey blades, silvery
buff/straw plumes.

## 2. Fescue — типчак (Festuca valesiaca, Volga/sheep fescue)

- Height: **0–0.51 m** (0–1.67 ft); the typical steppe form is short. Cultivar 'Nefer' **0.20–0.25 m**
  (8–10 in). — *USDA PLANTS profile FEVA2; BackyardGardener; Forestfarm 'Nefer'*
- Habit: densely tufted, sod-forming short grass; glossy pale blue-green hair-like flat leaves;
  sometimes semi-evergreen. — *BackyardGardener; USDA*
- Clump width: **NOT SOURCED** numerically (only "densely tufted / sod-forming"). Treat a single
  тип­чак tussock as a tight, narrow bunch — width is unverified, do not state a figure as fact.

Modeling note: low blue-green pincushion tussock, ~0.2–0.5 m tall, much shorter than ковыль; the two
together give the classic типчаково-ковыльная степь (fescue-feather-grass steppe) texture.

## 3. Meadow forbs (разнотравье)

- **Salvia pratensis (meadow sage / шалфей луговой):** basal clump **1.0–1.5 m** tall.
  — *Wikipedia "Salvia pratensis"*
- **Achillea millefolium (yarrow / тысячелистник):** **0.10–1.0 m** (10–100 cm), commonly to ~1 m;
  flat white/cream flower corymbs over fernlike foliage. — *Wikipedia "Achillea millefolium";
  NC State Extension Plant Toolbox*
- **Centaurea jacea (brown knapweed / василёк):** **NOT SOURCED** this run — no height figure was
  returned by the search; do not state a number as fact.

Colours: Salvia pratensis = violet-blue flower spikes; Achillea = white/cream (wild form) flat heads.

Modeling note: meadow forb layer for a fresh (lowland) meadow sits roughly **0.3–1.0 m**, with a few
emergent spikes (sage, knapweed) reaching ~1.0–1.5 m. Steppe разнотравье is generally shorter/sparser.

## 4. Carpathian rhododendron — Rhododendron myrtifolium (smirnia / бруслина)

- Shrub height: **0.30–0.50 m** (dwarf, dense evergreen) in the wild; cultivated to ~**0.60 m** (2 ft)
  and spreading **0.90 m+** (3 ft) wide. — *Missouri Botanical Garden Plant Finder; Plantiary;
  JARS v54n2 Czekalski (Carpathian species monograph)*
- Flowers: **rose to purple-pink**, tubular, in clusters of 3–7; rarely light pink or white.
  Bloom May–July. — *Missouri Botanical Garden; Plantiary; Bucegi Park flora*
- Leaves: **0.015–0.023 m** long (1.5–2.3 cm) × **0.005–0.008 m** wide (0.5–0.8 cm); dark green above,
  rusty (ferrugineous) scaly beneath. — *JARS v54n2 Czekalski; Missouri Botanical Garden*

Modeling note: low, dense, cushion/mound evergreen shrub ~0.4 m tall × ~0.9 m wide, smothered in
clusters of small rose-pink tubular bells. This is the alpine-zone Carpathian accent, NOT a steppe plant.

## 5. Common reed — тростник (Phragmites australis)

- Stem height: **2–4 m** (tallest in hot summers/fertile sites). — *Wikipedia "Phragmites australis";
  US Forest Service FEIS database*
- Stem (culm) diameter: **5–15 mm** (≈0.005–0.015 m), rigid, hollow, round, tan, unbranched.
  — *UConn Extension factsheet; US Forest Service FEIS*
- Habit: perennial from rhizomes/stolons; forms dense reed beds (live + standing dead stems);
  stands can exceed 1 km². Feathery purple-brown plume panicle at the top. — *Wikipedia; USGS NAS*

Modeling note: tall, vertical, unbranched canes 2–4 m with a thin (~1 cm) stem and a fluffy
buff/purple-brown seed plume; always in dense clusters at the water's edge.

## 6. Bulrush / club-rush — камыш (Schoenoplectus lacustris)

(True "bulrush"/club-rush — leafless round green stems, distinct from cattail.)
- Height: up to **3.5 m**. — *Wikipedia "Schoenoplectus lacustris"; NC State Extension*
- Stem diameter: **5–15 mm** (≈0.005–0.015 m), mid-to-dark green, not glaucous; most leaves reduced
  to bladeless sheaths. — *Wikipedia; NC State Extension*
- Habit: tufted, rhizomatous, grows in water **0.3–1.5 m** deep. — *Wikipedia; CANTUESO Seeds*

Modeling note: bundles of smooth, round, leafless dark-green spears to ~3.5 m, with a small brown
tufted inflorescence near the tip; stands in shallow open water.

## 7. Cattail / reedmace — рогоз (Typha latifolia, broadleaf)

- Height: **1.5–3.0 m**. — *Wikipedia "Typha latifolia"; Go Botany (Native Plant Trust)*
- Leaves: **0.02–0.04 m** broad (2–4 cm), flat, long, grass-like, alternate. — *Wikipedia*
- Flower spike: **0.15–0.20 m** long (15–20 cm) × **0.01–0.03 m** diameter (1–3 cm); dense cylinder,
  female (lower) + male tassel (upper). The brown "sausage" is the female section. — *Wikipedia;
  Brandeis field-bio species page*
- Habit: erect emergent from wet soil/shallow water; forms colonies. — *Wikipedia; WNPS*

Modeling note: the iconic brown velvet "cigar" (~0.15–0.20 m) on a stem, flanked by broad strap
leaves; whole plant 1.5–3 m. Distinct from камыш (round leafless stems) and тростник (plumed cane).

## 8. Boulder / rock moss

- **Grimmia pulvinata (cushion dry-rock moss):** cushion **~0.01–0.02 m** tall (1–2 cm), small grey-
  green hoary cushions on rock; capsules on curved setae. — *Wikipedia "Grimmia pulvinata"*
- **Grimmia orbicularis:** **0.01–0.08 m** (1–8 cm), dense cushions/mats several cm wide.
  — *Botanical Realm species page*
- **Leucobryum (cushion moss):** **0.03–0.10 m** high (3–10 cm); clumps from a few cm to ~1 m
  diameter. — *Encyclopaedia Britannica "cushion moss"*
- **Polytrichum (hair-cap moss):** often **0.15 m** or more; forms tussocks/wide beds (mostly in
  damp/peaty ground, not dry boulders). — *Encyclopaedia Britannica "hair-cap moss"*

Modeling note: for a boulder/rock surface use the Grimmia scale — a thin grey-green hoary cushion
**0.01–0.08 m** thick and a few cm to ~0.3 m across. Reserve the taller (0.1–0.15 m) cushion/hair-cap
mosses for damp shaded ground, not exposed steppe boulders.
