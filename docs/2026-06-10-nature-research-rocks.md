## 1. Rocks — boulders, outcrops, fieldstones

### Geological size classes (the defensible sizing backbone — Udden–Wentworth)
The exact size of a "boulder" vs "cobble" is a standardized geological class, not a guess. Use these
as the authoritative bins for procedural rock scattering.

- **Boulder:** rock fragment **> 0.256 m** (256 mm) in diameter. — *Wentworth scale / Wikipedia "Boulder"*
- **Cobble:** **0.064 – 0.256 m** (64–256 mm). — *Udden–Wentworth scale (Wentworth 1922)*
- **Pebble:** **0.004 – 0.064 m** (4–64 mm). — *Udden–Wentworth scale*
- Note the original 1922 search snippet quoted "boulders 250–100 mm"; that is a mis-statement — the
  canonical Wentworth boulder threshold is **>256 mm**, confirmed by Wikipedia. Use **>0.256 m**.

### Rounded mossy granite/gneiss stream boulders (Carpathian / Ukrainian-shield)
- Geology confirmed: **granite and gneiss are the basement rocks of the Ukrainian Crystalline Shield,
  and in river valleys their outcrops form a distinctive landscape.** — *Encyclopedia of Ukraine, "Granite"; Ukrainian Shield refs (Wikipedia / carpaty.net)*
- **Region-specific stream-boulder dimensions in metres: [UNSOURCED]** — no geological survey giving
  metre dimensions for individual Carpathian/Shield stream boulders was found. Model them using the
  Wentworth bins above: typical in-stream rounded boulders **~0.3–1.2 m** long axis (cobble-to-boulder
  range), large channel boulders to **~2 m**. (Sizing rationale = Wentworth class; the specific
  0.3–2 m spread is an engineering interpolation, not a cited regional figure.)
- Commercial "mossy granite boulders" (Australian landscape-stone catalog, for shape/mass intuition
  only — NOT a Carpathian source): individual pieces range **~500 kg to 3 tonnes**. At granite density
  ~2.65 t/m³ that is ~0.19–1.13 m³, i.e. roughly **0.6–1.3 m** equivalent diameter. — *Stone Life (stonelife.com.au) catalog*

### Rock outcrops
- **Specific outcrop footprint/height dimensions: [UNSOURCED]** — references confirm granite/gneiss
  outcrops exist along Ukrainian river valleys but give no metre dimensions. Treat outcrops as
  exposed bedrock masses (multi-metre, site-driven); do not commit a single sourced number.

### Fieldstones / glacial erratics (cleared from fields → clearance cairns)
- **Fieldstone (construction grade):** thickness **0.05–0.10 m** (2"–4"), face size "small dinner plate
  to serving platter" ≈ **0.20–0.45 m** across. — *Stoneyard, "What is New England Fieldstone"*
- **Glacial erratics span pebble → large boulder.** "Very large" erratics = **≥ ~0.9 m** (3 ft+)
  diameter; uncommonly large granitic erratics reach **≥ ~3 m** (10 ft+). — *Iowa PBS "Glacial Erratics"; Wikipedia "Glacial erratic"*
- Fieldstones are routinely removed from arable land and heaped into **clearance cairns** — a valid
  world-prop (a loose pile of 0.2–0.6 m stones). — *Wikipedia "Fieldstone"*

## 2. Fallen timber — logs, split logs, firewood stacks, stumps

### Fallen trunks / downed logs (coarse woody debris, CWD)
- **CWD diameter threshold:** a downed piece counts as "coarse" at **≥ 0.075 m** (7.5 cm) diameter in
  standard forest line-transect surveys (some authors use 2.5–20 cm). — *US Forest Service CWD transect method; Wikipedia "Coarse woody debris"; BC ESD field manual*
- **Diameter classes** used in surveys: typically binned every **0.05 m** (5 cm); "large material"
  = **> 0.10 m** diameter. — *USFS / MDPI Forests 12(7):881*
- **Length:** recorded to nearest **0.1 m**, measured from the large end down to the 0.075 m taper
  point. No single "average" length is standardized — fallen full trunks are effectively whole-tree
  length (commonly **~5–20+ m** depending on the tree). Long-axis length therefore is tree-driven, not
  a fixed cited constant. — *USFS CWD protocol*
- **Wood density (for mass / physics if needed):** ~0.28–0.71 Mg/m³ for >10 cm material depending on
  decay; 0.36 (2–5 cm) and 0.45 (5–10 cm) Mg/m³. — *Brazilian Amazon CWD study (ResearchGate 227605255)*

### Trunk / log diameter (drives log + stump diameter)
- DBH is measured at **1.30 m** above ground (international standard; 1.37 m / 4.5 ft in the US). — *Wikipedia "Diameter at breast height"; FAO 6.2*
- Worked example from sources: a **~0.61 m** (24-inch) DBH oak ≈ 120 years old. Harvestable hardwood
  minimum diameter ≈ **0.30 m** (12 in). — *Tree-age refs; NRS USDA "Diameter Limit Cutting"*
- **Eastern-European species-specific average DBH in metres: [UNSOURCED]** — no regional inventory
  figure found. Safe modelling range for mature trunks/logs: **~0.3–0.7 m** diameter (from the oak
  example + harvestable-minimum), with veterans larger; flag larger values as interpolation.

### Split logs (firewood billets) — ГОСТ 3243-88 "Дрова" (Soviet/Russian firewood standard)
This is the strongest sourced block — exact, era-correct Soviet numbers.
- **Standard billet lengths:** **0.25, 0.33, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00 m** (tolerance ±0.02 m). — *ГОСТ 3243-88*
- **Billet thickness (split/round cross-section):** **≥ 0.03 m** (3 cm) and up. — *ГОСТ 3243-88*

### Stacked firewood pile (поленница / штабель)
- **Stack width = the billet length** (a stack of 0.50 m billets is 0.50 m deep). — *ГОСТ 3243-88 / ГОСТ 9014.0 stacking rules*
- **Stack height:** measured as the arithmetic mean of ≥3 measurements; for stacks **> 10 m long**,
  height is sampled every **3 m**. — *ГОСТ 3243-88*
- **Long stacks (> 10 m)** must be braced with cross-stacked end cribs ("клетки") every **10 m**. — *ГОСТ 3243-88*
- **Green-wood shrinkage allowance:** wood > 25% moisture gets a non-counted addition of **+0.03 m
  per 1 m of stack height** (shrink/settle). — *ГОСТ 3243-88*
- **Складочный (stacked) cubic metre:** the trade volume unit is the **1×1×1 m stacked m³** (includes
  air gaps), the basis for поленница volume reckoning. — *Russian firewood trade refs (drova72.ru, vuoksa-wood.ru) re ГОСТ кубатура*
- **Specific "typical поленница" height/length in metres: [UNSOURCED as a fixed value]** — the standard
  fixes width (=billet length) and the measurement/bracing rules, not a single canonical height.
  Practical/stable build: depth 0.25–1.0 m (billet length), height ~1.0–2.0 m before bracing is
  required-ish, length arbitrary; the only HARD sourced trigger is the **10 m bracing rule**.

### Cut tree stumps (height + diameter)
- **Forestry definition:** stump diameter is measured over bark **immediately under the felling cut**;
  if stump height < **1.30 m** the measurement is taken at that height. — *FAO 6.2 "Tree height and diameter"*
- **Stump diameter** ≈ trunk base diameter (slightly > DBH): for mature trees roughly **0.3–0.7 m**,
  veterans larger — *derived from DBH sources above (NOT a regional cited figure → treat as interpolation)*.
- **Stump height (practical):** land-clearing operators deliberately leave tall stumps for leverage —
  small trees (0.08–0.20 m dia.) ≈ **~0.9 m** (3 ft); ~0.76 m (30 in) trees ≈ **~1.8 m** (6 ft+). — *Stump Removal Authority; TractorByNet land-clearing*
- A "large-diameter" stump in the trade = cut face **≥ ~0.61 m** (24 in). — *Stump Removal Authority*
- Note: clean mechanized/forestry felling normally leaves LOW stumps (commonly **~0.1–0.2 m**), but no
  single cited metre value was found in this pass — **[UNSOURCED exact]**; the tall-stump figures above
  are the cited ones (land-clearing context).

### Shattered / splintered war stumps (shell-blasted forest, e.g. WWI Western Front)
- Battlefield "camouflage trees" were built to imitate already-existing shattered war stumps and stood
  **3–6 m tall**, weighing several hundred kg — so the real splintered stumps they mimicked were of the
  same order, **~3–6 m** ragged trunks. — *History Skills "camouflage trees"; corroborated by IWM Somme / National WWI Museum descriptions of "splintered stumps"*
- Shape cues (sourced qualitative): jagged splintered crowns, no leaves/twigs, scorched, scattered wood
  splinters around the base, mixed with churned mud and barbed wire. — *IWM, National WWI Museum, Library of Congress "No Man's Land"*
- **Exact splinter-stump diameter/height distribution: [UNSOURCED]** beyond the 3–6 m height proxy.
