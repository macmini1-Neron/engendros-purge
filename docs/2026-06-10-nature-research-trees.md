# Region-Native Trees — Sourced Dimensions & Silhouettes (Ukrainian/Russian Landscape)

Research date: 2026-06-10. For ENGENDROS PURGE world plan + modelgen dossiers.
All dimensions in **metres**. Each fact carries a short source tag. Where a number
cannot be sourced, it is stated explicitly — nothing invented.

Scope: silver/downy birch (берёза), black/Lombardy poplar (тополь), Scots pine
(сосна обыкновенная), pedunculate oak (дуб), white willow (ива) + war-killed states.

---

## 1. Silver birch — Betula pendula (берёза повислая)

- **Mature height:** 15–20 m typical, up to ~30 m on good sites. [Van den Berk catalog; Woodland Trust]
- **Trunk diameter:** slender, usually **under 0.40 m** DBH. [Chew Valley Trees catalog]
- **Crown width:** ~6–9 m; slender, upright, open. [Van den Berk catalog]
- **Crown shape:** light, airy, narrow-ovoid to teardrop; thin canopy that lets light through.
- **Bark:** striking **white, papery, peeling**; develops **dark, diamond-shaped fissures**
  and a rough black base with age. [Woodland Trust; first-nature.com]
- **Branching habit:** slender twigs, often **pendulous / weeping** (hence "pendula");
  ascending main limbs, drooping fine shoots. [Woodland Trust; Wikipedia]
- **Foliage cluster:** small triangular doubly-serrate leaves; sparse, dappled,
  shimmering canopy; yellow in autumn. [Wikipedia]

### Downy birch — Betula pubescens (берёза пушистая) — the wetter-site sibling
- **Mature height:** 10–20 m, rarely to 27 m. [Wikipedia]
- **Trunk diameter:** up to **0.70 m**, exceptionally 1.0 m. [Wikipedia]
- **Bark:** **dull greyish-white**, smooth, marked with dark horizontal lenticels;
  less fissured / less papery than silver birch. [Wikipedia; rjtreesandhedging.co.uk]
- **Crown:** slender; twigs hairy, NOT warty, and NOT strongly pendulous (key difference
  from silver birch). [Wikipedia]

## 2. Poplar — Populus nigra (тополь чёрный) & cv. 'Italica' (Lombardy / пирамидальный)

### Black poplar — Populus nigra (the species)
- **Mature height:** commonly 20–30 m. [Wikipedia; Woodland Trust]
- **Trunk diameter:** typically up to **1.5 m** DBH; exceptional old French trees >3 m DBH. [Wikipedia]
- **Crown shape:** broad, spreading, often leaning, heavy low limbs; rugged.
- **Bark:** dark grey-brown, deeply fissured / rugged with bosses and burrs on old trees. [Wikipedia]
- **Branching habit:** massive arching limbs, irregular; **NUMBER for limb-angle not sourced — state as art-directed.**

### Lombardy poplar — Populus nigra 'Italica' (the iconic columnar roadside/windbreak tree)
- **Mature height:** up to ~30 m; commonly cited 12–21 m (40–70 ft), some to 21–27 m. [Gardenia.net; OSU Landscape Plants]
- **Crown width (spread):** very narrow — **3–4.5 m** (10–15 ft); columnar/fastigiate. [OSU; UF tree fact sheet]
- **Crown shape:** distinctive tall narrow **column / exclamation-mark**; branches steeply
  upswept hugging the trunk. [Gardenia.net]
- **Growth rate:** very fast, 2–3.6 m/yr under good conditions. [Gardenia.net]
- **Bark:** grey, becoming furrowed with age. [NC State Extension]
- **Silhouette note:** these line steppe roads, field edges and shelterbelts (полезащитные
  лесополосы) across Ukraine/S. Russia — the signature vertical accent of the landscape.

## 3. Scots pine — Pinus sylvestris (сосна обыкновенная)

- **Mature height:** up to ~35 m, exceptionally >45 m on productive sites; commonly ~25 m
  in stands, ~36 m max with lower branches shed. [Wikipedia; EU Forest Atlas]
- **Trunk diameter:** typically ~1.0 m DBH, exceptionally >1.7 m; generally **0.5–1.3 m**. [Wikipedia]
- **Crown shape:** **conical when young → flat-/round-topped, irregular, umbrella-like
  when old**, on a long bare straight trunk. [conifers.org; Trees for Life]
- **Bark — two-tone (signature):** lower trunk **thick, scaly-plated, grey-brown, deeply
  fissured**, plates up to ~5 cm thick; upper trunk + branches **thin, flaking, orange-red /
  copper**. [conifers.org; Wikipedia]
- **Branching habit:** lower branches self-prune with age leaving a tall clean bole;
  upper limbs widely spreading to ascending, holding the foliage as a high canopy. [Wikipedia]
- **Foliage cluster:** blue-green needles in pairs (2 per fascicle), 3–7 cm long, in
  dense tufts at branch ends; canopy reads as rounded dark-green clumps on bare red limbs. [Wikipedia]

## 4. Pedunculate / English oak — Quercus robur (дуб черешчатый)

- **Mature height:** 20–40 m; commonly cited ~30 m. [Wikipedia; Woodland Trust]
- **Trunk diameter:** very stout — old trees reach **2–4 m DBH**; girth (circumference)
  can exceed 10 m, reportedly to 14 m on pollards. [Wikipedia / Grokipedia]
- **Crown spread:** **15–25 m**, exceptional individuals >40 m; wide, spreading, rounded /
  domed. [Wikipedia]
- **Crown shape:** broad, heavy, dome-shaped; massive low gnarled limbs on open-grown trees.
- **Bark:** greyish-brown; smooth when young, becoming **thick, deeply / heavily fissured**
  with vertical ridges on old trees. [Woodland Trust; Wikipedia]
- **Branching habit:** thick, crooked, twisting boughs; characteristic gnarled, knotted
  silhouette; lower limbs heavy and near-horizontal on open-grown specimens.
- **Foliage cluster:** clustered lobed leaves on short stalks; dense, lumpy, dark-green
  canopy in heavy rounded masses. [Wikipedia]

## 5. White willow — Salix alba (ива белая / ветла)

- **Mature height:** 10–30 m; mature trees commonly to ~25 m. [Wikipedia]
- **Trunk diameter:** up to ~**1.0 m** DBH. [Wikipedia]
- **Crown width:** ~12–21 m (40–70 ft) depending on cultivar/site. [North Carolina Extension]
- **Crown shape:** **irregular, often leaning**, broad and somewhat open. [North Carolina Extension; Wikipedia]
- **Bark:** grey-brown, **deeply fissured** on older trees. [Wikipedia]
- **Branching habit:** ascending then arching limbs; long flexible drooping shoots
  (weeping in cultivars); fast-growing, short-lived (~full height in ~20 yr). [North Carolina Extension]
- **Foliage cluster:** narrow lance-shaped leaves with **silky white silvery hairs** giving
  the whole canopy a pale, silver-grey shimmer (the "white"/"alba"). [Wikipedia]
- **Habitat note:** riverbanks, pond edges, floodplains — the waterside tree of the region.

## 6. War-killed / battle-damaged states (front-line eastern Ukraine)

Qualitative, well-attested in reporting; **precise stump heights are NOT quantified in
sources — flag as art-directed, not sourced.**

- **General appearance:** intensively shelled forest becomes **"a field of stumps"** /
  **"stripped, broken trunks"** — described as a moonscape of **"black sticks"** and
  charcoal piles, no leaves. [Reuters/VOA "Ukraine's vast forests devastated"; Grist]
- **Snapped/shattered trunks:** trunks sheared and splintered by blast; **NO sourced metric
  for residual stump height** — recommend modelling a spread: ~2–6 m jagged snapped boles
  with splintered crowns (DESIGN VALUE, unsourced).
- **Charred / burnt extent:** widespread fire — **~5 million acres (~2 million ha) burned
  since 2022**, ignited by rocket fire, artillery and explosives; trees left **charred black,
  bark scorched, fully defoliated**. [Grist; Reuters]
- **Scale context:** Ukraine has ~10 million ha of forest; recovery estimated at "decades"
  and "billions of dollars". [Reuters]
- **Shrapnel scarring:** trunks pocked/gouged by fragmentation and embedded metal — widely
  noted qualitatively; **no dimensional source.**

### Modelling recommendation (DESIGN, not sourced)
- Bare snapped trunk: keep base diameter per species above; truncate at a jagged break;
  splinter spikes at the top. Snap-height range ~2–6 m suggested.
- Charred state: blacken bark texture, remove all foliage, optional thin grey ash tone at base.
- Mix three damage tiers in a war zone: intact, defoliated/scarred, snapped+charred stump.
